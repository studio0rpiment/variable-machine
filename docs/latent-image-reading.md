# The latent image: reading list

The texts for the latent image card, gathered 2026-11-09. The card's plan (`docs/latent-image-plan.md`, section 4) lists the public-domain texts first brought in. This list adds:

- the critique of Freud;
- Jung, and the critique of Jung;
- contemporary dream science;
- writing on images and memory;
- texts from outside Europe, starting with Korea and West Africa.

**How the texts enter the card.** As in the plan, the texts don't change the model: the encoder is given pictures only. They frame the latent image. The student reads them, chooses passages, and prints them beside the latent image on the gallery page, each with its source.

**What the access column means:**

- **Public domain:** free to download, quote and bring into the tool (`data raw gutenberg <number> <name>`).
- **Open (licence):** free to read online under the licence named. Quote with credit; check NC (no commercial use) and ND (no changed versions) before reusing.
- **Library:** an academic book or article. A student reads it through the university library and quotes it as research (short passages, with citation). It isn't copied into the tool or the repository.

Links were checked on 2026-11-09. Licences marked "check" were not visible on the page and should be read on the PDF itself.

## 1. Freud, and the critique of Freud

Freud's dream theory is the thesis's starting point: condensation, his comparison of a dream to Galton's composite photographs, and the dream as wish. Most dream researchers since the 1950s have rejected its main claims. The latent image should be read with that critique, not as an illustration of Freud.

| Text | What it says | Access |
|---|---|---|
| Freud, *The Interpretation of Dreams* (Brill translation, 1913) | Condensation; the Galton comparison | Public domain: Gutenberg 66048 |
| G. William Domhoff, *The Neurocognitive Theory of Dreaming* (MIT Press, 2022), doi:10.7551/mitpress/14679.001.0001 | Tests Freud's claims, and the other main theories, against dream-content research. Finds Freud's major claims don't hold up, while crediting him with the idea that dreams reflect the dreamer's concerns | **Open: CC BY-ND**, whole book as PDF from MIT Press |
| Domhoff, "Why did empirical dream researchers reject Freud? A critique of historical claims by Mark Solms", *Dreaming* 14 (2004): 3–17 | The history of the rejection, against Solms's defence of Freud | Library; a copy may be in Domhoff's DreamResearch.net library (dreams.ucsc.edu/Library) |
| Adolf Grünbaum, *The Foundations of Psychoanalysis: A Philosophical Critique* (University of California Press, 1984) | The standard philosophical critique: psychoanalysis's evidence comes from the clinic, where the patient's agreement can be suggested by the analyst | Library |
| Karl Popper, "Science: Conjectures and Refutations", in *Conjectures and Refutations* (1963) | Psychoanalysis as a theory that no observation could refute | Library |
| Mark Solms, *The Hidden Spring* (Profile, 2021) | The contemporary defence: a neuroscientist arguing some of Freud's ideas survive in brain science | Library |

## 2. Jung, and the critique of Jung

Jung's **collective unconscious** is a layer of the mind shared by everyone, holding **archetypes**: inherited patterns that show up as images in dreams and myths. It is the idea nearest to the latent image, and the most dangerous to borrow without its critique. A latent image is made from one collection; it isn't a shared, inherited store of images.

| Text | What it says | Access |
|---|---|---|
| C. G. Jung, *Psychology of the Unconscious* (1912; Beatrice Hinkle's translation, 1916) | Jung's break with Freud: myth and dream images as transformations of one psychic energy | **Public domain** (US): Gutenberg **65903**. Jung died in 1961, so the German original is in copyright where the term is life + 70, until 2032 |
| Farhad Dalal, "Jung: A Racist", *British Journal of Psychotherapy* 4, no. 3 (1988): 263–79 | Jung's equation of "primitive" states of mind with "primitive" peoples, and that this runs through his theory | Library (PEP-Web) |
| Andrew Samuels, "Comments on Farhad Dalal's 'Jung: A Racist'", *BJP* 4 (1988): 280 | A Jungian's reply: accepts the evidence, argues "nation" rather than "race" drove Jung | Library (PEP-Web) |
| "Open Letter on the Question of Jung's Writings and Theories about 'Africans'", *BJP* 34, no. 4 (2018) | Thirty years after Dalal: Jungian analysts asking the field to acknowledge Jung's writing on Africans | **Open**: on the Black, African and Asian Therapy Network site (baatn.org.uk) and jungiananalysts.org.uk |
| Frantz Fanon, *Black Skin, White Masks* (1952), chapter 6 | Jung's collective unconscious describes "the childhood of Europe", not of the world. Fanon keeps the idea only as the images a culture teaches, such as the colonial images of Black people | Library (Fanon died in 1961) |
| Komarine Romdenh-Romluc, "Fanon, the recovery of African history, and the nekyia", *European Journal of Philosophy* (2022) | How Fanon turns a Jungian idea (the descent, *nekyia*) against Jung | **Open**: author's copy on White Rose eprints (eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/194052) |

**For the card:** Fanon is the reading that fits the project best. A collection's latent image is made of the images a person has absorbed from their culture, so it can show those learned images rather than a universal unconscious.

## 3. Contemporary dream science

| Text | What it says | Access |
|---|---|---|
| Erik Hoel, "The overfitted brain: Dreams evolved to assist generalization", *Patterns* 2, no. 5 (2021), doi:10.1016/j.patter.2021.100244 | Dreams as the brain's own noise: strange, out-of-pattern images that keep it from **overfitting** to the day. Overfitting is a model learning its examples so closely that it fails on new ones. The argument comes straight from machine learning | **Open: CC BY-NC-ND 4.0** (PubMed Central PMC8134940; preprint arXiv:2007.09560) |
| Domhoff (above) | Dreams as embodied simulations of the dreamer's waking concerns, measured by content analysis | Open: CC BY-ND |
| J. Allan Hobson and Robert McCarley, "The brain as a dream state generator: an activation-synthesis hypothesis", *American Journal of Psychiatry* 134 (1977): 1335–48 | Dreams as the forebrain making sense of random brainstem activity: no hidden wish | Library |
| Antti Revonsuo, "The reinterpretation of dreams: an evolutionary hypothesis of the function of dreaming", *Behavioral and Brain Sciences* 23 (2000): 877–901 | Dreams as threat rehearsal | Library |

**For the card:** Hoel is the strongest link between dream science and what the tool does. The trained decoder overfits a little to the collection; "what it brings" is close to the out-of-pattern image Hoel describes. That is an analogy, and the card should call it one.

## 5. Beyond Europe

### Korea

Korean writing has a long tradition of the dream as a whole life seen and woken from, and of dreams that can be given, bought or read before a birth.

| Text | What it says | Access |
|---|---|---|
| Kim Man-jung (Kim Man-choong), *Kuunmong* (*The Cloud Dream of the Nine*, 1687), translated by James Scarth Gale (London: D. O'Connor, 1922) | A monk dreams a whole worldly life and wakes to find it a dream. The first Korean literary work translated into English | **Public domain**: Internet Archive `cu31924023333077`; also at Eldritch Press (ibiblio.org/eldritch/kim/cloud9.html). Not on Gutenberg, so `data raw gutenberg` can't fetch it; download the text from the Archive |
| Iryeon, *Samguk yusa* (*Memorabilia of the Three Kingdoms*, 1280s): the tale of the monk Josin (Chosin), who dreams fifty years of married life in one nap | The source of the "whole life in a dream" form that *Kuunmong* takes up | Library: Frits Vos et al., *Vestiges of the Three Kingdoms of Ancient Korea* (University of Hawai'i Press, 2025); older translation by Ha Tae-hung and Grafton Mintz (Yonsei University Press, 1972) |
| *Samguk yusa*: Munhui buys her elder sister's dream, and so becomes queen | A dream as something owned and traded; I didn't check the passage in a translation | Library, as above |
| **Taemong** (태몽), the conception dream: a dream, by the mother or a relative, that announces a child and its nature | Dreams read as images of a person before they exist | Library: Fred Jeremy Seligson, *Oriental Birth Dreams* (Hollym, 1989), the one book-length English study I found. Wikipedia's "Conception dreams" points to Korean encyclopedia sources |

### West Africa

In much West African thought, a dream isn't a private message from the dreamer's own unconscious. It is a social event: a visit from ancestors or spirits, a call to an office, knowledge given to a diviner. That is a strong counterweight to Freud and Jung.

| Text | What it says | Access |
|---|---|---|
| M. C. Jedrej and Rosalind Shaw, eds., *Dreaming, Religion and Society in Africa* (Leiden: Brill, 1992) | The standard collection: dreaming in sickness and healing, craft, conversion, and election to religious office | Library |
| Rosalind Shaw, "Dreaming as accomplishment: power, the individual and Temne divination", in Jedrej and Shaw (1992) | Among the Temne of Sierra Leone, diviners gain standing through accomplished dreaming | Library |
| Peter McKenzie, on Yoruba dreams and visions in the 19th century, in Jedrej and Shaw (1992) | How Yoruba dreaming changed, and what continued, through conversion | Library |
| Keith Ray, "Dreams of grandeur: the call to the office in northcentral Igbo religious leadership", in Jedrej and Shaw (1992) | Dreams as the call to religious office | Library |
| Genevieve Nrenzah, "Dreaming and Reality: The Concept of Dayeʔ/ʔlaleʔ Dreams in the Akan Worldview", *International Journal of Dream Research* 18, no. 2 (2025): 169–178 | Interviews with Nzema and Asante Akan people (Ghana): the dream as part of a web of relations with others, the living and the dead | **Open** (the journal is open access); licence: check the PDF |
| Simon Charsley, "Dreams of power: social process in a West African religious movement", *Africa* (International African Institute, 1983) | Dreams in a West African church movement | Library (Cambridge) |
| Jacob K. Olupona and Rowland Abiodun, eds., *Ifá Divination, Knowledge, Power, and Performance* (Indiana University Press, 2016) | Ifá, the Yoruba system of divination and its body of verse. I found no chapter on dreams; it is background for how Yoruba knowledge is read from signs | Library |

I found no open-access, peer-reviewed article on Yoruba dreams in particular. The Jedrej and Shaw chapters are the place to start, with the library.

## 6. What to bring into the tool

The public-domain texts, with the command that fetches each:

```
data raw gutenberg 66048 freud-dreams
data raw gutenberg 65903 jung-unconscious
data raw gutenberg 59709 zhuangzi
data raw gutenberg 11562 galton-inquiries
```

*Kuunmong* is public domain but isn't on Gutenberg: its text is on the Internet Archive (`cu31924023333077`).

The open-licence works are read online and quoted with credit, as are the library ones:

- Domhoff, CC BY-ND;
- Hoel, CC BY-NC-ND;
- Zylinska, CC BY-SA;
- Nrenzah, licence to check.

None of them is copied into the repository.
